# Preamble

The rate of automation is unmatched nowadays. As a student who entered the AI concentration of Computer Science in 2023–shortly after the commercialization of ChatGPT in late 2022–I was dropped right into the middle of this paradigm shift. Still, from 2023 through late 2024, I was fortunate enough to actually use my brain to study CS, rather than outsourcing all my thoughts like I’ve caught myself doing recently.

I’ve realized that I use AI most effectively in moments when I still have to struggle on my own. An example would be a Computer Networking project I built at the end of 2024: [Remote Control Via Email](https://sabertoaster.github.io/projects/remotecontrolviaemail/). I leveraged AI massively for this, mainly to draft boilerplate code and suggest my next steps. However, the inherent friction of the project–wrestling with Gmail integrations, `windows.h` API and unfamiliar surfaces–kept my thinking anchored. I felt intensely present, and the engineering experience felt highly original. I remember that one time I need to parse the webcam image to gmail but utterly failed in the process of doing it no matter what since low level C++ lib didn’t allow that (or I was just noob) and that felt great in comparison to frictionless work of mindless prompting. Skipping ahead just a few months, I decided to embark on a completely different topic: implementing deep learning models. I rallied up a [Flower Classification](https://sabertoaster.github.io/projects/resnet50fellowship/) project in just a few days. The problem? I had absolutely no fundamentals going into it. I outsourced all of the implementation and theoretical understanding to the AI. On a superficial level, the model’s performance was quite good. But internally, I didn’t get anything out of it. It horrendously surfaced my lack of genuine understanding. Looking back, I felt a profound disgust with myself for bypassing the learning process, and I swore never to "vibe" on that hollow high again.[^1]

One project made me feel unusually present because the friction of real systems forced me to think. The other moved blindingly fast but completely hollowed out my understanding. That contrast is what this post is really about.
